# Premise — Your Diwali, as a receipt (30 s)

Spine: (myth) Diwali ka bill = jitna dukaan ne bola. (mechanism) Receipt pe GST ek alag line hai, rate cheez pe depend karta hai.
(so what) Dry fruit 5% [F2], gold jewellery 3% on gold + making together [F3]. (in ₹) ₹2,000 box → ₹100, ₹50,000 set → ₹1,500, ₹1,600 total [F1].
(do) Bill pe GST line padho.

Candidates:
1. The receipt is the only one in the family who tells the truth at Diwali — mummy and Fufaji say a version; the receipt prints the real line. Heightens: mummy's "bas ek box" → Fufaji's WhatsApp myth → the total nobody said aloud.
2. Sarkar is the silent guest at every Diwali purchase, eating a different-size piece of every plate. (Guest/doorbell game taken 11-07 — too close.)
3. GST is the "aura tax": every gift earns you aura and the sarkar a cut. (Slang carries it; rate logic unclear.)
4. The receipt as a Diwali greeting card from the sarkar, signed in tiny print. (One joke, not three steps.)
5. The gift box is a relay baton across three houses; the tax is paid once. (True, but the concept is the line, not circulation.)
6. Receipt as the family WhatsApp group: 40 greetings and one useful message nobody reads. (Stacks with #1.)
7. Mummy's list has 3 items, the receipt has 4 — the fourth is sarkar. (One beat.)
8. The receipt as a class monitor reading out names. (Teaches nothing new.)

Pick: #1. True when retold ("the receipt shows what nobody says out loud — the GST line, and its rate changes by item"), it debunks a real myth (5% on making, F3 note) through Fufaji, and the callback lands on a self-own: I threw the only honest one out with the gift wrap.
