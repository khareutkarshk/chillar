# Premise — free trial autopay (30 s)

## Spine (no jokes)
1. A free trial asks you to set up a UPI AutoPay mandate: one PIN that permits recurring debits [F6].
2. The trial ends; the mandate doesn't, so the debits keep coming.
3. The bank must notify you at least 24 h before each debit, with merchant, amount, date [F2][F3].
4. Recurring debits up to ₹15,000 don't need a fresh PIN [F5], so nobody asks you again.
5. Fix: UPI app → Autopay / Mandates section → pause or revoke [F7]; the cancel needs PIN/OTP [F4].

## 8 candidate games
1. **"Raat 2 baje wala main"** — past-me at 2 AM is a reckless guy who signs permissions day-me pays for. Heighten: he typed the PIN → he ignored the bank's 24 h warning → he did it six times. Payoff: same PIN, typed awake.
2. The free trial is a guest who came "sirf ek raat" and is still living here in October. Heighten: pays himself rent, brings 5 friends. (Stock "guest who never leaves".)
3. The mandate is a milkman told "roz daal dena" who keeps delivering after you moved. Clear, but no self-own and the image is dated.
4. The mandate is a hostel friend who borrowed "₹199, agle mahine" and now just takes it. Asked once, never again (F5). Good, but money-lending games are taken (credit card).
5. The trial is a first date that's free; the mandate is the marriage. Dating game is taken (credit-enquiry).
6. The SMS is the bank whispering a warning in a horror film nobody listens to. One step only.
7. Autopay as a gym membership you pay to not use. Shape taken (minimum-due example).
8. The free trial as a "lucky draw" scam where you're the one who wins the bill. Scam framing overstates — it's legal.

**Pick: #1.** It *is* the mechanism (one PIN = ongoing permission), it's a self-own the send-to friend
recognises, and it heightens in three true steps (PIN → ignored notice → six times = ₹14,328) with a callback
(the same PIN, typed awake). F8 (RBI→NPCI review) is unverified; skipped per facts notes.
