# Premise — Bhai Dooj gift tax (30 s, Myth vs Math)

Spine: Myth — "behen ko cash diya toh gift tax" [F1]. Mechanism — gifts from non-relatives are totalled for the
year; above ₹50,000 the whole sum is taxed [F1]. So what — the rule doesn't apply to relatives at all [F3]
(bhai-behen, bhabhi, mama-mausi [F4]); the real risk is cousin/dost money crossing ₹50,000 [F5]. In ₹ — ₹37,100
from bhai-bhabhi-mama = ₹0 [F6]; ₹60,000 from cousin + dost = the whole ₹60,000 [F7]. New Act from April 2026 [F2].

Candidates (game → how it heightens):
1. **Tax is the function-wali mausi who only asks "yeh kaun hai?"** — bhai → "apna hai", pass; cousin → mausi says family, tax says outsider; total crosses ₹50,000 → poora lifafa. Callback: "yeh kaun hai?"
2. Society guard with a register: family walks in unwritten, outsiders get logged, register crosses ₹50,000 → whole register goes. (true, but less Bhai-Dooj, weaker act-out)
3. The ₹50,000 rule is a shaadi gate with a guest list. (shaadi muddles with the separate marriage exemption — cut)
4. Behen's tika thali as a scanner that beeps only on outsiders. (gimmick, one step only)
5. The tax law is a family-tree chart on the wall. (correct but no voice, no laugh)
6. Lifafa as a bouncer checking IDs. (bouncer = stock)
7. Behen's "bas itna?" vs taxman's "itna kyun?" (amount-centred — teaches the wrong model: amount isn't the test for relatives)
8. Cousin as the villain in a family serial. (punches at a person who did nothing wrong)

Pick: #1. The question the rule actually asks is the one every aunty asks at a function — so the metaphor
is the mechanism (relation decides, not amount). Three true steps: relative = zero, cousin = "bahar wala",
cross ₹50,000 = whole amount. Retold to a parent, it's still correct.
