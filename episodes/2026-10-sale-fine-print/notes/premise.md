# Premise — sale fine print (30 s, ₹100 vs ₹1,00,000)

## Spine (no jokes)
1. A sale banner says "10% instant discount*".
2. The asterisk hides three conditions: one specific card only, minimum cart ₹5,000, maximum discount ₹1,500 [F1].
3. A ₹100 cart gets ₹0 — 0% — because it is under the minimum [F4].
4. A ₹1,00,000 cart gets only ₹1,500 because of the cap — 1.5%, not ₹10,000 [F4].
5. The headline 10% is true only between ₹5,000 and ₹15,000; read the cap before believing the %.

## 8 candidate games
1. **The asterisk is the offer's lawyer** — banner promises, the tiny star reads out the conditions; heightens card-only → minimum → cap, and "settles" ₹10,000 down to ₹1,500.
2. Sale as a club with bouncers — card at the door, minimum spend for entry, drink limit inside. Heightens, but three bouncers = three characters; muddier.
3. Wedding chachu: "result jitna bada, gift utna bada" — then the envelope is capped. Clear for the cap, weak for card/minimum.
4. Friend's "treat meri taraf se" with rules revealed at the bill. Funny but the friend is a stock prop.
5. Dating profile with old photos — the 10% is the filtered photo. Explains "misleading", not the mechanics.
6. Gym "up to 50% off" hedge-words — overlaps M2 but explains only "up to".
7. Exam marking scheme — "10 marks*, *only if you use blue pen, write 5 pages, max 1.5". Clear, but school-ish and long.
8. The banner as a guy who never lies, just never finishes his sentence. Close to #1, less visual.

## Pick: #1 — the asterisk is the offer's lawyer
Each condition is the lawyer speaking (card → you're out; minimum → ₹100 gets 0%; cap → ₹1,00,000 gets 1.5%), so the
escalation *is* the math, and the payoff calls back the banner: it didn't lie, it handed the truth to the star.
