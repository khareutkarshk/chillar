# Premise — 2026-10-26-report-mdr-payer

## Spine (no jokes)
1. NPCI says MDR on select UPI merchant payments starts after Diwali; no date yet [F1].
2. MDR = 0.4% on P2M payments above ₹2,000; ≤ ₹2,000 free [F3].
3. The merchant pays it; he can't add it to your bill — you pay the posted price [F4]. ₹3,000 sale → he gets ₹2,988, you pay ₹3,000 [F8].
4. ~96% of merchant payments unaffected; small merchants up to ₹1 lakh/month pay zero [F6].
5. The fee goes to banks/UPI apps for the rails — not a tax, not the government [F5].

## 8 candidate games
1. **Wrong-address panic: the whole country is opening an envelope addressed to the shopkeeper.** Heightens: it's his bill (F4) → you pay the same ₹3,000 (F8) → most shops won't even get the envelope (F6). ← PICKED
2. Free chutney with samosa: free for you, the shop pays the chutney supplier. Heightens weakly; chutney/samosa = generic desi prop.
3. Road toll that only trucks pay; passengers panicking at the booth. Clear, but not desi-specific, no act-out.
4. The Soundbox personified, announcing ₹3,000 while the shop gets ₹2,988. Needs a mechanism no source states.
5. Restaurant service-charge fight, reversed: this time the bill can't reach your table. Needs too much setup.
6. Hostel mess fee charged to the warden, students striking anyway. Fun, but one step only.
7. Reading someone's notification over their shoulder in the metro and getting upset. Small; folds into #1.
8. "Diwali ke baad dekhte hain" as India's national policy, now official. Great single joke (used in setup), not a full game.

Why #1: "who pays" IS the explanation; the envelope image escalates with each fact and pays off as a callback (the group-chat forward is the only envelope addressed to you).
