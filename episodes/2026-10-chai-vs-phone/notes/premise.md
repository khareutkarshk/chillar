# Premise — chai vs phone (round 1)

## Spine (no jokes)
1. To judge an expense fairly, convert it to a yearly cost.
2. ₹100 a day on chai = ₹100 × 365 = ₹36,500 a year (example) [F1].
3. A ₹1,00,000 phone replaced every 2 years = ₹50,000 a year (example) [F2].
4. So the phone costs ₹13,500 more per year than the daily chai [F3].
5. Rule: har kharche ko saal mein badlo, phir judge karo.

## 8 candidate games
1. **The forward's formula is right, it's just aimed at the wrong cup** — the "chai chhodo" logic (×365) is valid; run it on the phone you read the forward on and it convicts the phone. Heightens: chai blamed for savings → ghar → shaadi; I obey it; then the same formula on my phone.
2. Courtroom: chai is the usual suspect, phone has an alibi ("kaam aata hai"). Heightens via evidence, but courtroom is a generic frame.
3. Chai and phone as two flatmates splitting rent — phone pays less per day "on paper". Confusing for the yearly lens.
4. Phone as a silent EMI-paying ghost. Drifts into EMI/interest — off-concept.
5. Tapri bhaiya as my accountant. Funny but the math doesn't live in him.
6. The phone secretly drinks chai (₹137 a day). Strong single beat — use as a heightening step inside #1.
7. Diet logic: counting chai calories while eating a whole cake. Metaphor outside money; one-off.
8. Crime show "Villain kaun?" with a twist reveal. Twist works but the game is just the format.

**Pick: #1.** It *is* the concept (the yearly lens is the forward's own method, applied evenly), escalates in three true steps (blame grows → I comply → formula hits my phone), punches at the advice and at me, and pays off with a callback (the villain is the phone the forward arrived on; tapri bhaiya gets his customer back).
