# Premise — 2026-11-05-card-swipes-bill

## Spine (no jokes)
1. Myth: a ₹100 card tap feels like UPI — no OTP, no PIN, no pain — so it feels like nothing went out.
2. Mechanism: a swipe is the bank's money lent to you, with a free window up to the due date [F2].
3. So what: clear the *entire* bill by the due date and no interest is charged [F2]; miss it and the free period is lost and interest may run from the swipe date [F3]; while old balance is pending, new swipes get no free window either [F5]; minimum due only stops it being called "overdue", it doesn't clear the bill [F4].
4. In ₹: maan lo ₹10,000 of Diwali swipes [F11] → paid in full = ₹0 extra; left unpaid, RBI's own example (2% a month) ≈ ₹250 for one month [F12].
5. Do: read "total amount due", not "minimum due", and know the due date.
- takeaway: Card ka tap udhaar hai; poora bill due date tak bharo toh byaaj zero.
- follow_up: "Byaaj kitna lagta hai?" → RBI fixes no number; each bank sets its own rate — read yours [F8].

## 8 candidate games
1. **The card is the kirana uncle's khata** — every tap is a line in his register; close the whole khata by the date and it's free; miss it and he counts byaaj back to the first chai. Heightens: chai line → Diwali page totals ₹10,000 → he backdates byaaj to the chai + "₹ thoda de do, late nahi ginunga".
2. Card tap = free trial of the bank's money; forget to "cancel" (pay in full) and it bills you from day one. Heightens: trial chai → trial kurta → auto-renew backdated. (Paying ≠ cancelling — map wobbles.)
3. "Baad Mein" is a real person; every tap invites him; he arrives on the due date with the bill. Heightens: invited once → he brings friends → he charges rent from day one. (Fun, less true-when-retold.)
4. Swipes are relatives you ghosted; the bill is all of them arriving at once on Diwali. (Only the total, not the mechanism.)
5. Minimum due is a hostel warden who signs your late-entry slip but doesn't let you out. (Covers min due only.)
6. The card limit is a stranger's fridge you think is yours. (Teaches "limit ≠ balance", not the free window.)
7. Card is a cricket "trial ball" — taken (cricket game taken 2026-11-04). Rejected.
8. Card is a very polite moneylender who never says no — that's comedy.md's own example. Rejected.

**Pick: 1, the khata.** It is literally what a card is (udhaar, written down, settle by a date), papa already speaks khata, and it heightens on facts: line → page → backdated byaaj. Retold alone ("card ek khata hai, date tak poora chukao toh free, warna byaaj pehli chai se") it is still correct.
