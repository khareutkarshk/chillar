# Premise — Diwali gifting maths

## Spine (no jokes)
1. One dabba feels cheap, so nobody prices the whole list.
2. The real bill is box price × number of households: maan lo ₹600 × 30 = ₹18,000 (F4, F5, F6).
3. The list rarely shrinks, because gifting is reciprocal: whoever gave last year expects one back.
4. The GST cut on dry fruits (12% → 5%, F1) is real, but it cuts the rate, not the length of the list.
5. Flip the maths: fix the total first, divide by the list: ₹12,000 ÷ 30 = ₹400 per house (F7).

## 8 candidate games
1. **"Ek hi dabba toh hai" is the most expensive sentence of Diwali.** True once, said thirty times → list grows → it's udhaar so it can't shrink → tax cut, list didn't → callback with ₹400.
2. The list is a wedding guest list nobody approved: it only grows. Heightens: relatives → neighbours → strangers. (Weaker on the maths.)
3. Mithai is a loan book: every box received is an EMI due next Diwali. (Good step 2, but no clear step for the multiplication.)
4. Soan papdi as a currency circulating the mohalla. (Fun, but teaches recycling, not budgeting.)
5. Family WhatsApp group as a budget session where nobody tables the total. (Too close to politics framing.)
6. The dabba as a status symbol / aura farming arms race. (One step, then flat.)
7. The list as autoplay: "next episode" — each name triggers the next. (Overlaps 2.)
8. Kaju katli price-per-gram detective. (Needs facts we don't have.)

**Pick: #1.** It *is* the concept (one price × the list), it has three true steps that get worse (₹18,000 → list can't be cut because it's udhaar → govt cut tax but nobody cut the list), and the payoff calls back the hook sentence with the fixed per-house number.
