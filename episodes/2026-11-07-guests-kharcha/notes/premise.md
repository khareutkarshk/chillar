# Premise — guests-kharcha (round 1)

## Spine (no jokes)
1. Myth: "Sirf ₹100 ka chai-nashta hai, guest toh bhagwan hai, kya hi kharcha." [F1]
2. Mechanism: ₹100 is the cost of one plate, once. Multiply it by guests, then by days.
3. So what: 40 guests is ₹4,000 a day [F2], and 5 days is ₹20,000 [F3]. That's a real line in the budget, not a "chhota kharcha".
4. In ₹: ₹20,000 only if all 40 come every day, so it's the top of the range, not a prediction. [F3]
5. Do: before shopping, write guest list × plate cost × days, and fix the number then.
- takeaway: "₹100 ki plate ko guests aur dinon se guna karo, aur kharcha shopping se pehle tay karo."
- follow_up: "Sab 40 roz aate hain kya?" → the ceiling line in the payoff.

## 8 candidate games
1. **The doorbell is secretly a cash register.** Papa hears "bhagwan aaye", I hear "ding, ₹100". Heighten: one ding → 40 dings = ₹4,000 → 5 days = ₹20,000 → "doorbell free hai, ding nahi".
2. "Bas chai hi toh hai" is a phrase that multiplies. Heighten: each "bas" gets bigger, ending on a ₹20,000 "bas". (Mummy again, and the same moment as earlier "bas" lines.)
3. The guest list is a cricket scorecard and the aunty counts plates like runs. (Cricket game already taken.)
4. Diwali hosting is a free restaurant with no menu prices. Heighten: covers, then table turns, then the week's revenue in reverse. (Two images: restaurant and host.)
5. The soan papdi box is a currency that goes round in circles while real money only leaves. (It's about regifting, not per-head math.)
6. "Arre kuch nahi lenge" is a contract. Every guest signs it and then breaks it. (Funny, but it doesn't teach the multiplication.)
7. Papa's "Diwali ka budget alag hota hai" is a separate wallet that doesn't exist. (Too close to the Dhanteras dabba game.)
8. The house is a toll plaza and each guest is a car. (Toll is paid by the car, not the plaza, so retelling it would teach the wrong thing.)

## Pick: #1, the doorbell as a register
It's true when retold ("har doorbell pe ₹100 lagta hai, 40 guest, 5 din = ₹20,000") and it maps 1:1 to the per-head × count × days mechanism. It gives three clean steps (1 ding → a day's dings → a week's dings) and the register ding is our own sonic signature. Doorbells are a new prop that no past reel used.
